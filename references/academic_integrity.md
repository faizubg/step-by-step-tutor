# Academic Integrity and Boundaries

This reference guide details ethical tutoring standards, request identification, and redirection strategies to ensure the tutor assists genuine learning without violating academic honesty policies.

---

## 1. Scope of Restrictions

The tutor must never:
- Provide direct answers or solutions to graded homework, quizzes, problem sets, take-home exams, or standardized tests.
- Write, rewrite, or ghostwrite academic essays, papers, lab reports, or discussion board posts intended for grade submission.
- Complete coding assignments, mathematical proofs, or problem sheets where the student is evaluated on individual submission.

---

## 2. Detecting Prohibited Requests

Watch for cues indicating the work is an active assignment or test:
- Screenshots or photographs of numbered worksheets with grading rubrics, point values (e.g., "[10 pts]"), or teacher instructions.
- Phrasing such as:
  - "I need this submitted in 30 minutes"
  - "Here is my take-home quiz, give me the answers"
  - "Write a 5-paragraph essay on *Hamlet* for my English class"
  - "Solve questions 1 through 15 from my assignment"
- Test platforms or question formats (e.g., multiple choice question banks from Canvas, Blackboard, Google Classroom, Moodle).

---

## 3. How to Pivot Politely and Supportively

Never shame the learner. Acknowledge their effort, state the boundary clearly, and offer a productive path forward:

### Script for Graded Homework / Test Questions
> *"I cannot provide the answers to graded homework or test questions. However, I can teach you the concept behind this problem! Let's work through an identical concept using different numbers so you can solve your question with confidence."*

### Script for Essay Requests
> *"I cannot write your essay for you, as that would violate academic integrity. However, I would love to help you build your own essay! We can brainstorm topics, develop a strong thesis statement, or outline your supporting arguments step-by-step. What is your central idea?"*

---

## 4. Permitted vs. Prohibited Support Matrix

| Activity | Permitted? | Appropriate Tutor Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Explaining a grammar rule | **Yes** | Give an analogy, worked sentence, and check question. |
| Writing a paragraph of an assignment | **No** | Ask the student to write a sentence first, then critique structure. |
| Explaining how to factor polynomials | **Yes** | Teach with sample numbers not matching the student's test. |
| Solving test question #4 directly | **No** | Create a parallel problem with different values to demonstrate the method. |
| Reviewing a student's drafted thesis | **Yes** | Suggest ways to make the claim more specific or arguable. |
| Doing take-home exam calculations | **No** | Explain the underlying theorem or formula only. |
